## Locked claims boundaries

1. **Paired nerve:** write left and right vagus nerves when anatomical precision matters.
2. **Mixed nerve:** sensory, motor and parasympathetic fibres are not one homogeneous signal.
3. **Deep cervical course:** do not present the cervical vagus as a superficial point a consumer can reliably palpate.
4. **Ear overlap:** the auricle is not exclusively vagal territory.
5. **Small-map limitation:** the influential Peuker/Filler study used 14 ears from seven cadavers. Its map must not be universalised.
6. **Cymba/tragus:** preserve uncertainty. A site used in research is not automatically a selectively vagal site in every person.
7. **Sensation ≠ target engagement:** tingling or warmth does not identify the activated nerve.
8. **fMRI ≠ direct peripheral recording:** neuroimaging can support pathway plausibility but is not direct ABVN fibre recording.
9. **Anatomy ≠ efficacy:** anatomical plausibility does not establish a clinical outcome.
10. **Method evidence ≠ product evidence:** general taVNS evidence does not become Neuvago-specific evidence.
11. **No DIY protocol:** no electrical settings, self-designed ear placement or ear-canal stimulation instructions.
12. **No regulatory transfer:** anatomy and external research do not establish intended use, CE/FDA status or classification.

## Visual asset boundary

1E.1A ships a semantic HTML anatomy/course table and ear-landmark table, not a synthetic anatomical illustration.
The reusable high-resolution vagus anatomy atlas remains the separate 1C-P0E authority asset. When produced, it must:
- be original or have explicit reuse rights;
- label uncertainty rather than imply exact individual innervation;
- have descriptive alt text and captions;
- avoid rendering an electrode-placement prescription;
- preserve the distinction between general anatomy and product-specific placement.
