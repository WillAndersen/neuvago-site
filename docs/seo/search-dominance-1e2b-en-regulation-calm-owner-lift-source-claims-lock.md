## Locked interpretation boundaries

1. “Nervous system regulation” is a useful explanatory framework, not a single formal diagnosis or one measurable biological state.
2. Regulation is not equivalent to constant calm.
3. The autonomic nervous system is only part of whole-body regulation.
4. Sympathetic and parasympathetic activity do not form one universal slider.
5. The vagus nerve is not the entire parasympathetic system and not the whole regulation system.
6. Heart rate alone cannot diagnose a whole-body autonomic state.
7. HRV can sample aspects of cardiac autonomic regulation under defined conditions, but is not a direct whole-ANS or whole-vagus meter.
8. Proprietary wearable “stress” or “readiness” scores are not universal clinical measures of nervous-system regulation.
9. Slow breathing can influence cardiorespiratory physiology and vagally mediated HRV, but this is not proof that the whole vagus nerve has been “activated.”
10. Relaxation techniques can support a relaxation response, but no technique is guaranteed to work for every person.
11. Acute exercise evidence is outcome-specific and does not justify a claim that movement instantly “resets” the nervous system.
12. Feeling calmer does not prove “parasympathetic dominance.”
13. Calming practices do not diagnose or treat a specific medical or psychiatric condition on these pages.
14. Relaxation techniques are not a substitute for needed medical or mental-health care.
15. General physiology, breathing, relaxation or stress research is not Neuvago product evidence.
16. These pages must not claim Neuvago reduces sympathetic activity, increases parasympathetic activity, raises HRV, activates the vagus nerve, or “regulates” the user without direct product evidence.
17. No medical-review badge or named clinical reviewer is introduced.
18. No new URL, redirect or canonical change is allowed in 1E.2B.
