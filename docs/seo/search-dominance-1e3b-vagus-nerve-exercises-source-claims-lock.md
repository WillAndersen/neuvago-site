## Locked claims

Allowed:
- Behavioural practices such as slow breathing, movement and relaxation can influence subjective experience and selected physiological/autonomic outcomes under studied conditions.
- Slow-paced breathing has a clearer direct literature for heart rate and HRV-related measures than many viral “vagus hack” claims.
- Exercise and movement can influence stress-reactivity and cardiac-autonomic outcomes, with results varying by intervention, population and outcome.
- Relaxation, meditation and gentle yoga can be described as broader behavioural regulation practices with heterogeneous evidence.
- HRV is an indirect, context-sensitive cardiac measure and is not a direct whole-vagus test.
- Electrical VNS and behavioural exercises are different method categories.

Not allowed:
- Any statement that a behavioural exercise has been proven to directly and selectively stimulate the whole vagus nerve.
- Any “reset,” “heal,” “activate on command,” “tone the vagus,” or guaranteed parasympathetic-switch claim.
- Any claim that a specific HRV change proves target engagement, vagus strength, vagus damage, or treatment response.
- Any transfer of implanted VNS, tVNS, taVNS or other electrical-device evidence to breathing, movement, yoga, humming, gargling, tapping, cold exposure or Neuvago.
- Any improvised electrical electrode placement, electrical settings, carotid/neck pressure manoeuvre, medical protocol or personalised suitability recommendation.
- Any product efficacy claim, condition-treatment claim, diagnosis, symptom score, health-input workflow or personalised exercise prescription.

## Viral-claim boundary

Humming, singing, gargling, tapping, cold exposure and pressure-point routines may be named only to explain why they require a separate evidence check. They are not presented here as proven vagus exercises. The planned `/learn/vagus-nerve-evidence-vs-internet-claims` hub owns the systematic evaluation of those claims.

## Safety boundary

The page may advise readers to keep ordinary breathing, movement and relaxation comfortable and appropriate to their context, and to stop/reduce effort with marked dizziness, chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness, new neurological symptoms or another worrying reaction. It must direct acute or serious symptoms to appropriate urgent/emergency care and persistent/worsening concerns to qualified assessment.

The page cannot determine suitability for a specific reader. Product-specific electrical stimulation remains governed by its exact instructions, intended use, contraindications, precautions and stop-use guidance.
