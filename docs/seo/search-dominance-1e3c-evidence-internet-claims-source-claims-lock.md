## Locked claim matrix

### Slow breathing
Allowed:
- Slow-paced breathing can change heart rate and vagally mediated HRV measures under studied conditions.
- Breathing-related HRV changes are compatible with altered cardiac parasympathetic modulation.

Not allowed:
- A claim that HRV proves direct, selective stimulation of the whole vagus nerve.
- A claim that a breathing count diagnoses vagus strength or guarantees a health outcome.

### Humming / singing
Allowed:
- Small studies report changes in breathing pattern, HRV or autonomic measures during humming.
- Humming has measurable respiratory/nasal effects, including altered sinus ventilation and nasal nitric oxide.
- Respiratory pacing and vocalisation are plausible contributors to observed autonomic changes.

Not allowed:
- A claim that humming vibration is proven to selectively stimulate the vagus nerve.
- A claim that humming is a validated vagus reset, treatment or universal protocol.

### Gargling
Allowed:
- The pharynx participates in cranial-nerve and airway-protective reflexes involving multiple pathways.
- Controlled oropharyngeal stimulation can change cardiovascular and sympathetic responses.

Not allowed:
- A claim that ordinary gargling is established as a direct vagus-stimulation or parasympathetic-reset protocol.
- Instructions that deliberately provoke gagging as a wellness technique.

### Cold exposure / face cooling
Allowed:
- Cold-water, face-cooling and cryostimulation paradigms can change heart rate, blood pressure and cardiac autonomic measures.
- Some controlled paradigms show increased cardiac vagal/parasympathetic indices during exposure.
- Effects vary by exposure type, temperature, immersion, timing and individual context.

Not allowed:
- “Cold resets the vagus nerve.”
- A claim that stronger or colder exposure produces more useful vagus stimulation.
- A prescriptive cold-plunge temperature, duration or face-immersion protocol.
- Transfer of athletic recovery or diving-response findings into a general therapeutic claim.

### Tapping / EFT
Allowed:
- EFT has been studied as a psychological intervention and systematic reviews report promising symptom outcomes with methodological limitations.
- Outcome evidence can be described at the level of the intervention actually studied.

Not allowed:
- A claim that EFT/tapping works by proven vagus-nerve stimulation.
- A claim that symptom change proves vagus target engagement.

### Neck pressure / carotid sinus
Allowed:
- Carotid-sinus massage is a defined clinical diagnostic manoeuvre for selected syncope evaluation.
- Clinical use includes monitoring, technique standards and exclusion criteria.

Not allowed:
- A universal vagus pressure point.
- Neck coordinates, pressure grades, timing sequences or DIY carotid-sinus instructions.
- Converting a monitored clinical manoeuvre into a consumer wellness exercise.

## Cross-cutting prohibited claims

The new hub must not state or imply:
- guaranteed vagus activation, reset, healing or “toning”
- direct target engagement inferred from warmth, tingling, yawning, sighing, calm, pulse change or one HRV reading
- diagnosis of a weak, damaged, overactive or underactive vagus nerve from self-observation
- transfer of implanted VNS, tVNS, taVNS or Neuvago evidence to behavioural practices
- product efficacy, disease treatment, prevention, cure, diagnosis or personalised suitability
- improvised electrical placement/settings, carotid/neck manoeuvres or cold-exposure dosing

## Safety boundary

The hub is educational and does not prescribe any of the claim-checked methods.

It must:
- keep carotid-sinus and strong neck-pressure manoeuvres in clinical/safety-sensitive territory
- avoid cold-plunge or face-immersion dosing
- avoid deliberate gagging instructions
- avoid improvised electrical stimulation
- direct serious or worrying medical symptoms to appropriate professional/urgent care
